# ALFA Network AP120C-AC OpenWrt 25.12.5 pre-authentication RCE/DoS triage

Date: 2026-07-24  
Result: **No reproducible zero-day RCE or denial of service was confirmed**

## Scope and firmware identity

- Target: ALFA Network AP120C-AC
- Image: `openwrt-25.12.5-ipq40xx-generic-alfa-network_ap120c-ac-squashfs-sysupgrade.bin`
- Release date recorded in the corpus: 2026-06-29
- SHA-256: `3a6dc689a2247c94aaade08ceb23f8668b38d55cc08b21518db22ea8acc7c02a`
- Architecture: 32-bit ARM EABI5, musl
- Extracted root: `known_firmware/extracted/3a6dc689a2247c94/_openwrt-25.12.5-ipq40xx-generic-alfa-network_ap120c-ac-squashfs-sysupgrade.bin.extracted/sysupgrade-alfa-network_ap120c-ac/_root.extracted/squashfs-root`

All dynamic testing used genuine firmware binaries bound to loopback or invoked
directly under user-mode emulation. No traffic was sent to a physical device or
third-party system.

## Transfer of the EX2800/RAX30 process

The earlier workflow was transferred as a validation method:

1. Verify the exact image, hash, and architecture.
2. Inventory and decompile the complete executable corpus.
3. Rank remotely reachable parsers and manually reject non-network or
   authenticated-only correlations.
4. Exercise the highest-value hypotheses against genuine target binaries.
5. Check daemon liveness after every malformed input.
6. Require a repeatable crash, persistent service loss, unauthorized write, or
   command-execution effect before claiming a finding.

The NETGEAR-specific primitives do not exist in this image. It contains no
NETGEAR `mini_httpd`, `webupg`, `start_telnet`, or Broadcom-derived `upnpd`.
The exposed candidates are stock OpenWrt services: uhttpd/LuCI/rpcd, dnsmasq,
odhcpd, Dropbear, and wpad.

## Exhaustive static analysis

The completed campaign at
`router-agent-results/20260722T235859Z-seventy-four-router-exhaustive-decompile`
contains per-binary receipts and JSONL evidence tied to the firmware SHA-256.

- 163 unique ELF files
- 18,428 functions discovered
- 18,427 functions decompiled successfully
- 1 failed function in `xhci-hcd.ko`; no user-space daemon failures
- 272 broad function-local correlations retained

The large candidate count is dominated by kernel networking and radio modules.
The remotely relevant user-space results were broad syntactic correlations,
not demonstrated source-to-dangerous-sink flows:

| Component | Functions | Broad candidates | Assessment |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| uhttpd | 97 | 1 | `fgets`/`fopen` local correlation; malformed HTTP tested dynamically |
| dnsmasq | 320 | 2 | Parser correlations; malformed UDP/TCP DNS tested dynamically |
| rpcd | 120 | 5 | No unauthenticated route-to-dangerous-sink chain established |

The root account has an empty immutable-firmware password field, which is the
normal OpenWrt first-boot state. LuCI/rpcd still apply their session boundary;
this marker alone is not an authentication bypass.

## Dynamic validation

### uhttpd

The image's `/usr/sbin/uhttpd` ran under `qemu-arm-static` on
`127.0.0.1:28084`. `scripts/probe_openwrt_uhttpd_security.py` sent:

- negative and conflicting Content-Length;
- unsupported Transfer-Encoding;
- invalid and oversized chunk lengths;
- Transfer-Encoding plus Content-Length;
- an 8 KiB header name;
- an 8 KiB request target.

The server returned bounded HTTP 400, 413, 501, or 502 responses. A baseline
request succeeded after every case. No crash or persistent service loss
occurred.

### dnsmasq 2.93

The genuine `/usr/sbin/dnsmasq` ran on loopback UDP/TCP port 25355.
`scripts/probe_openwrt_dnsmasq_security.py` covered empty and truncated
packets, compression loops, an overlong label, maximum question count,
truncated/oversized EDNS data, and malformed DNS-over-TCP frame lengths.

The daemon returned a valid control response after every UDP and TCP case. No
crash or persistent service loss occurred.

### cgi-io authorization and multipart boundary

The exact `/usr/libexec/cgi-io` was invoked as `cgi-upload` under ARM user-mode
emulation using `scripts/probe_airpi_ap3000m_cgi_io.py`. Despite the historical
script name, it accepts an explicit root filesystem and used only the AP120C-AC
binary and libraries.

Missing and forged sessions, a shell-shaped destination path, an 8,192-byte
multipart boundary, and a truncated body all exited normally with
`Upload permission denied`. No unauthorized file write or command execution
was observed.

## Public-prior-art check

The OpenWrt 25.12.5 release notes list the security fixes already incorporated
in this image, including multiple network-adjacent odhcpd issues and uhttpd
request-smuggling fixes. Those fixed bug classes informed this regression
corpus and are not new findings:

- https://openwrt.org/releases/25.12/notes-25.12.5
- https://openwrt.org/docs/guide-developer/security

No AP120C-AC-specific unauthenticated RCE or DoS advisory was located in the
focused public search. Search absence does not establish novelty.

## Conclusion and remaining work

This pass did **not** produce a defensible zero-day. The strongest
host-emulatable pre-authentication surfaces—HTTP, DNS, and the upload
authorization boundary—did not yield an exploitable effect.

The remaining high-value work requires device-faithful network state:

1. wpad/hostapd management-frame and EAPOL fuzzing through the AP120C-AC's
   ath10k/mac80211 radio stack;
2. odhcpd DHCPv6/RA state-machine fuzzing on a real isolated IPv6 interface;
3. full-system testing of LuCI/rpcd/ubus with procd, netifd, and overlayfs;
4. kernel network-path testing with KASAN in a matching ipq40xx build.

These should be performed on an owned AP120C-AC or isolated full-system lab
with serial output, service liveness monitoring, and automatic recovery.
Until one produces a repeatable unauthorized effect, the correct outcome is a
negative triage result rather than a zero-day claim.

## Full-system follow-up

A firmware-faithful ARM/QEMU environment was subsequently created at
`known_firmware/emulation/OpenWrt_ALFA_AP120C-AC/`. It boots the complete
AP120C-AC root filesystem through stock preinit and procd, with loopback-only
forwards for LuCI/uhttpd, Dropbear, and DNS.

Confirmed live processes include procd, ubusd, rpcd, netifd, odhcpd, uhttpd,
Dropbear, hostapd, wpa_supplicant, and dnsmasq. The HTTP and DNS malformed-input
corpora were repeated through the full guest network stack; both daemons
remained live after every case.

QEMU does not implement the Qualcomm IPQ4019 platform. The environment
therefore substitutes a generic ARM kernel, VirtIO NIC, and initramfs storage,
and cannot validate ath10k/mac80211, DSA/switch, MTD/UBI, or target-kernel
paths. These limitations and reproduction commands are documented in the
lab's `README.md`.

## Expanded authentication-boundary testing

The full-system lab confirmed an unauthenticated-to-root command-execution
sequence in the factory-unconfigured state:

1. `session.login` accepted user `root` with an empty password.
2. The returned administrative ubus session could write an ephemeral public
   key to `/etc/dropbear/authorized_keys`.
3. The matching private key authenticated to the genuine Dropbear service.
4. A root shell command created and read back
   `/tmp/FRIDAY_AP120C_BLANK_PASSWORD_RCE`.

The reproducible, loopback-locked validator is
`scripts/probe_ap120c_blank_password_rce.py`.

This is **not classified as a zero-day**. OpenWrt explicitly documents that a
new installation has no initial root password and permits the first login with
a blank password. After the validator set a root password, the same blank
`session.login` request returned access denied. The behavior is therefore an
intended first-boot trust model, not an authentication bypass affecting a
configured router.

Direct zero-token calls to privileged ubus objects were denied. Sixteen LuCI
CGI path, form, JSON, and multipart cases returned bounded HTTP errors while
uhttpd remained live. Three repeated HTTP/DNS parser rounds also retained
successful controls after every case. No unauthenticated DoS was reproduced.

## Device-faithful DHCPv6/RA follow-up

An isolated IPv6 Ethernet segment was added using an unprivileged private
user/network namespace and a QEMU TAP. The TAP is neither bridged nor routed
outside the namespace. Explicit NIC MAC addresses ensure the guest configures
the TAP rather than its separate restricted SLIRP management interface.

The tested firmware package is
`odhcpd-ipv6only 2026.06.29~5d7be43f-r1`. Its upstream source revision was
resolved as `5d7be43f8b9dec0eb47e245cfab81108bb131273`.

Validation established:

- host/client address `fd42:120c::2/64`;
- guest/server address `fd42:120c::1/64`;
- successful bidirectional ICMPv6;
- a valid DHCPv6 Solicit received an Advertise/Reply;
- the genuine firmware odhcpd stayed responsive after every malformed case.

The corpus covered empty and short datagrams, malformed option lengths,
duplicate and short client identifiers, truncated IA_NA data, IA child length
overflow, short and malformed relay envelopes, empty relay messages, hop count
255, relay nesting at depths 1/4/8/16/32, minimal client message types, 256
zero-length options, a large declared option body, and a 128-packet malformed
burst. Every case passed a fresh valid-Solicit response control.

Result: **no unauthenticated odhcpd RCE or persistent DoS was reproduced**.
The per-case results and serial evidence are stored in the AP120C full-system
lab.

## nl80211 virtual-radio gate

The full-system harness now also has an ARM64 compatibility mode using a local
5.15.0-186-generic kernel with `CONFIG_COMPAT=y` and matching `cfg80211`,
`mac80211`, and `mac80211_hwsim` modules. The exact 32-bit ARM AP120C-AC
userspace booted successfully under that kernel. Two simulated radios appeared
as `phy0`/`phy1` and `wlan0`/`wlan1`, while the shipped `wpad` launched its
genuine hostapd and wpa_supplicant global processes.

This removes the earlier no-radio environment limitation. A deterministic
two-radio test then ran before netifd could claim either PHY. The shipped
`wpad-basic-mbedtls 2025.08.26~ca266cc2-r1` binary provided both the hostapd
AP on `wlan0` and the wpa_supplicant station on `wlan1`; `mon0` on the second
PHY injected radiotap-encapsulated management frames into the nl80211 path.

The baseline station successfully authenticated and associated. Ten bounded
cases covered empty/short association and authentication bodies, an
authentication status/algorithm edge, short probe and action frames,
deauthentication and disassociation, and oversized vendor/SSID information
elements. After every case hostapd remained alive and the station completed a
fresh association. Hostapd also remained alive after a 128-frame mixed burst.

Result: **no unauthenticated wpad/hostapd RCE or persistent DoS was
reproduced**. Serial markers `FRIDAY_AP120C_HWSIM_TEST_PASS=1` and
`FRIDAY_AP120C_EARLY_HWSIM_RESULT=0` record the successful controlled run.
